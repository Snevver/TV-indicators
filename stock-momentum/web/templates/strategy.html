What this thing actually does

Once a month it ranks forty large American companies by how much they rose over six months, ignoring the most recent month, and holds the top eight in equal amounts. That is the whole idea.

Why skip the last month

Shares that jump hard tend to give some of it back over the next few weeks. Ranking on the six months ending a month ago steps around that short-term reversal, and it is the difference between a rule that works and one that does not.

Why eight

Fewer names means the ranking matters more and one bad pick hurts more. Eight out of forty is concentrated enough that the ranking drives the result, spread enough that no single company decides your year.

What it costs you to run

Most months two or three names change. Across twenty-one years of backtest the median month kept five or six of the eight, and only five months in that whole span required no trade at all. Budget for two to six orders a month.

The part worth deciding about in advance

The worst stretch in the backtest was a 59.7% drawdown bottoming in March 2009 — deeper than the index fell. The account did not see its December 2007 high again until May 2013. Five and a half years of opening this page and seeing a number lower than one you already had.

2009 is the specific weakness. The strategy made 9% while the index made 23.5%, because coming off the bottom the names with the best six-month record were the defensive ones that had fallen least. Momentum buys what has already worked, which is exactly the wrong thing to hold on the day the market turns. That is not a bug that got fixed. Expect it to happen again.

How the money moves at a rebalance

Total the whole account, divide by eight, make every position that size. A stock you sell at a profit does not fund its replacement — the gain is spread across all eight, which is why every position grows together. Nothing is ever profit set aside; the gains are the account.

Paper and Trading 212

The paper book simulates the strategy on assumed fills and keeps running whatever else happens. The live book mirrors what Trading 212 actually holds. Keeping both means the gap between them is measurable, and that gap is your execution: slippage, currency fees, an order placed late or skipped. Without it you could not tell a bad month from a badly executed one.

What the backtest cannot tell you

The forty names were chosen in 2026, so every historical figure is flattered by knowing which companies survived. Holding all forty and never trading beat the strategy over twenty-one years — but 41% of that result was NVIDIA alone, and nobody picking a list in 2005 would have held it through an 85% fall. Against the index, which you could actually have bought, the strategy turned $1,000 into $36,110 versus $6,233.
